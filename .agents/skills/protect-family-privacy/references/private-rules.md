# Private publication rules

Treat this file as private configuration. Never quote protected source values in user-facing output.

## Child identity

- The child's legal name is protected in every public surface.
- Public/editorial replacement: `Bababou`.
- Official/legal-derived replacement: `the child`, `the daughter`, `l’enfant`, or `ma fille`.
- Do not store the protected legal-name variants in tests or generated audit output.

## Parents in official/legal-derived content

- Father: use `the father`, `le père`, or `MR AA`.
- Mother: use `the mother`, `la mère`, or `Mme RB`.
- Outside official/legal-derived content, the father's intentionally public identity and contact information may remain public.

## Institutions and professional contacts

Institutions, organizations, professionals/staff, their professional identities and professional email addresses may be named. Relevant historical map/location links may remain public. Do not remove them solely because they relate to the case.

## Address

- Never disclose the exact private home address outside an existing explicitly scoped exception.
- Replace it with the approved generalized location from `.cursor/mem/privacy-address-phone.md` when required.
- Detect formatting variants without logging the matched value.

## Phone/contact

- Distinguish private phone data from deliberately published site contact data.
- Contact/email surfaces may expose the father's approved public contact information.
- Do not use a blanket phone rule to make Bababou contact pages diverge unnecessarily from the public contact pattern of the companion site.

## Medical and psychological data

Medical or psychological diagnoses, records, treatment, medication, assessments, therapy and equivalent sensitive health data remain protected unless a later explicit publication exception is given.

## Existing signature exception

Address/phone transformations do not apply to existing files under `public/assets/nabla/signature/`. Keep that exception confined to the directory. Child legal-name and medical/psychological protections still apply.
