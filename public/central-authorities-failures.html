Central Authorities Failed: A Father in Europe Could Not Have His Child Located or Returned

Summary of institutional failures across three countries — no location of the child, return refused or not registered, years of delays.

In short

  • No authority located the child. France, Norway and Germany did not locate the child; the father had to find her himself in Germany.
  • Norway refused return. Oslo District Court refused the petition for return under the Hague Convention (2016).
  • Germany refused to register the return request. The German Central Authority did not want to register the father’s request for return to France; the French authority initially said it was “not competent” for a displacement Norway→Germany.
  • Years of delay. Central Authorities took months or years to answer; procedures restarted when the child was moved to another country.

Failures by country

France

  • Years of delay before meaningful action (e.g. contact with German authority only in 2026).
  • Initially treated the case as “not competent” for displacement Norway→Germany; father had to insist on return to France.
  • No location of the child by French authorities.

Reaching Central Authorities

Norway

  • Oslo District Court refused the petition for return (2016) on Convention grounds (habitual residence, Art. 3, 12, 13, 20).
  • No location of the child by Norwegian authorities; father had no effective remedy to have the child returned.

Abduction in Norway

Germany

  • Central Authority refused to register the return request (contradiction with visitation, Art. 12 delay, request to withdraw).
  • German authority “had no trace” of a 2022 return request; assumed return was to Norway, not France.
  • No location of the child by German authorities; father located the child himself in Frankfurt.
